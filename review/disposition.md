## Disposition

- **Which state encodes one photon?** The revised title and abstract identify the single-sector extension under test. Section 3 distinguishes one physical input label from the $N$ auxiliary excitations generated by the published product of derivatives. A single collective derivative gives odd parity in every sector and zero vacuum weight. Antigravity's follow-up reviewed [revision 9024c15](https://github.com/Quantyra/cwl-replica-parity/blob/9024c15a6691cfaf1e579bdb566792816b887fc8/manuscript.typ) and maintained its editorial objection. The subsequent draft explicitly tests the alternative on an ordinary passive pulse. A collective input and collective output with the unchanged root give equal limiting weights even when the ordinary probabilities are $3/4$ and $1/4$. This identifies the additional readout change that a different encoding would require. A preferred physical embedding and general detector instrument remain open.
- **Same physical comparison across years.** Section 4 separates the product on identical numerical inputs from the preparation specified in each primary paper. The 2022 line-state overlaps and the correlated Gaussian in the 2024 calculation need not agree in higher sectors. Only the common $N=1$ parity zero is required to evaluate the literal product. No equality of full experiments, regulating order or detector probabilities follows.
- **Sharpness and robustness.** Section 6 isolates the off-return vacuum factor under the same common quadratic model. For relative and collective pair coefficients $a,A_c$, terms in $\sum_i x_i^2$ vanish from the square-free derivative. If both vacuum prefactors are nonzero the even subsequence tends to $\lvert C_r\rvert^2\lvert A_c-a\rvert/e$, which persists locally around the return for fixed positive $h$. Odd-sector zeros also extend to symmetric endpoints of even total per-line occupation. Proving the even limit positive for every such endpoint would require a separate calculation.
- **Measurement and source scope.** The revised manuscript specifies the mechanical basis and displaced optical field. Subsequence choices, infinite-outcome summability, optical loading/readout losses and an alternate detector basis are explicit limitations. No experimental impossibility bound or demand for a unique physical encoding follows. Eq. (121) in the optical paper differentiates once in every replica even in its passive example. The replicated sector there carries $N$ total incident excitations. We retained that source convention.

- **Bright coefficient bound.** Grok suggested a factor of $(k+1)$ in the geometric majorant. Expanding the diagonal coefficient gives the sum over even binomial indices with central-binomial factors $b_j=\binom{2j}j/4^j\le1$. Bounding each $b_j$ by one and adding the nonnegative odd-index terms gives $R^k$ directly. Section 5 now proves the original sharper bound. In the vacuum argument the single factorial $(2m)!$ comes from the square-free monomial in the power of the sum. The displayed finite amplitude in the review uses the correct single factor.
